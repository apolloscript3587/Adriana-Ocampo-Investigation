# Current Role and Institutional Source Reconciliation

**Cut-off date:** 13 October 2026

## Finding

Do not describe Adriana Ocampo as “currently working at NASA” without qualification. The public record contains a meaningful freshness discrepancy:

- NASA Science's official profile labels her “Scientist — NASA Headquarters” and says the page was last updated on **5 November 2024**.
- UNESCO's COMEST directory was updated on **25 September 2026** and calls her a former NASA Science Program Manager, former New Frontiers Lead Program Executive, and former Discovery Program executive.
- The UNESCO directory lists her as a **Courtesy Research Professor** in the University of Central Florida (UCF) Department of Physics and Astronomy and **Vice-President of COMEST for 2026–2027**.
- UCF's own faculty listing independently identifies her as a **Courtesy Research Professor**.
- The Florida Division of Corporations lists the Stelpolvo Ocampo Uria Foundation, Inc. as active and identifies Adriana C. Ocampo as a director/officer; UNESCO describes her as founder and president of the foundation.

### Best current wording

> Dr. Adriana C. Ocampo Uría is a Colombian-born planetary geologist and former NASA science-program executive. As of October 2026, current institutional listings identify her as a Courtesy Research Professor at the University of Central Florida and Vice-President of UNESCO's COMEST for 2026–2027, as well as founder and president of the Stelpolvo Ocampo Uria Foundation.

This wording is based on the latest available institutional listing, not a direct statement by Ocampo announcing a departure date. It should be updated if NASA or Ocampo publishes a more recent official employment record.

## Interpretation

A profile can remain online after an employment change; “live page” does not mean that every biographical detail is current. The NASA bio is still an authoritative first-person source for birthplace, childhood, education, and her own account of Chicxulub. For current position, the more recently updated UNESCO listing and UCF's current directory take precedence.

The available evidence supports a transition from NASA program leadership into academic and international science-ethics work. It does not, on its own, establish the exact date her NASA employment ended. This dossier therefore does not assign a retirement date.
