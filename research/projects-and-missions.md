## 1. JPL planetary mission coordination

UNESCO's current profile lists Ocampo's earlier work at JPL as a science coordinator for missions including Viking, Mars Observer, Voyager, Galileo and others. This is consistent with NASA's biography describing her early move from volunteer work to employment at JPL while completing university degrees.

These programs span key phases of robotic planetary exploration. The record supports describing her as a mission-science professional who worked in a major NASA laboratory. It does not currently support a complete timeline with exact start/end dates or precise technical tasks for every mission; that would require personnel records, mission staff directories, and archival project documents.

Sources: [NASA biography](https://science.nasa.gov/people/adriana-ocampo/); [UNESCO COMEST directory](https://www.unesco.org/fr/ethics-science-technology/comest?hl=es-PE).

## 2. New Frontiers: New Horizons, Juno and OSIRIS-REx

NASA en Español's 2022 profile says Ocampo led three New Frontiers missions: New Horizons, Juno and OSIRIS-REx. The UNOOSA/APOSCOS program for a 2022 space-cooperation meeting identifies her as New Frontiers Lead Program Executive with responsibility for those missions. The UNESCO directory in 2026 records her as a former New Frontiers lead executive.

The New Frontiers program uses competed missions to pursue focused planetary-science objectives. A lead program executive helps oversee the programmatic conditions under which a mission is developed and carried out. The role involves the bridge between science goals, management, schedules, resources, institutions, and accountability. It is not the same as serving as the spacecraft's principal investigator or claiming sole credit for its scientific observations.

### New Horizons — Pluto and the Kuiper Belt

NASA states that New Horizons was the first spacecraft to explore Pluto up close in July 2015 and later flew past the Kuiper Belt object Arrokoth in January 2019. These were major milestones in the exploration of the outer Solar System. Ocampo is associated with the mission through her documented New Frontiers program-executive role.

- Mission source: [NASA Science — New Horizons](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/new-horizons/)
- Role source: [NASA en Español biography](https://ciencia.nasa.gov/gente-de-la-nasa/dra-adriana-ocampo-uria-explorando-el-universo-e-inspirando-en-la-tierra/), [UNOOSA/APOSCOS meeting PDF](https://www.unoosa.org/documents/pdf/copuos/stsc/2022/APSCOSymposiumDuringSTSC-ProgramOutline10Feb2022.pdf), and [UNESCO COMEST directory](https://www.unesco.org/fr/ethics-science-technology/comest?hl=es-PE).

### Juno — Jupiter

NASA's Juno mission page says the spacecraft entered orbit around Jupiter on 4 July 2016. The orbiter studies Jupiter's origin and evolution, the planet's interior and atmosphere, and the broader formation of the Solar System. Ocampo's documented connection is program-executive leadership in New Frontiers rather than an attribution of specific Juno discoveries to her individually.

- Mission source: [NASA Science — Juno](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/juno/)
- Role source: [UNOOSA/APOSCOS meeting PDF](https://www.unoosa.org/documents/pdf/copuos/stsc/2022/APSCOSymposiumDuringSTSC-ProgramOutline10Feb2022.pdf) and [NASA en Español biography](https://ciencia.nasa.gov/gente-de-la-nasa/dra-adriana-ocampo-uria-explorando-el-universo-e-inspirando-en-la-tierra/).

### OSIRIS-REx — asteroid Bennu

NASA describes OSIRIS-REx as a sample-return mission to asteroid Bennu. It collected material on 20 October 2020 and delivered its return capsule to Earth on 24 September 2023. NASA later reported that the returned material totaled 121.6 grams (4.29 ounces). Its sample can help scientists investigate the early Solar System and the inventory of materials associated with life's origins. Ocampo's connection is program leadership in New Frontiers, not personal collection or analysis of all returned material.

- Mission overview: [NASA Science — OSIRIS-REx](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/osiris-rex/)
- Return milestone: [NASA — OSIRIS-REx delivers NASA's first asteroid sample to Earth](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/osiris-rex/osirisrex-delivers-nasas-first-asteroid-sample-to-earth/)
- Role source: [UNOOSA/APOSCOS meeting PDF](https://www.unoosa.org/documents/pdf/copuos/stsc/2022/APSCOSymposiumDuringSTSC-ProgramOutline10Feb2022.pdf).

## 3. Discovery program and Lucy

The UNESCO profile updated in September 2026 lists Ocampo as both a former New Frontiers lead executive and a former Discovery Program executive. Its French-language parenthetical groups Lucy, Juno, and New Horizons near the Discovery entry, which is ambiguous when compared with the more explicit 2022 UNOOSA biography: that document identifies Juno, New Horizons, and OSIRIS-REx as her New Frontiers portfolio. NASA separately identifies Lucy as its 13th Discovery Program mission. To avoid carrying a possible classification error forward, this dossier treats Lucy as Discovery and Juno, New Horizons, and OSIRIS-REx as New Frontiers, while preserving the ambiguity in the current UNESCO entry as a source limitation.

NASA describes Lucy as the first spacecraft mission to explore Jupiter's Trojan asteroids, primitive bodies thought to retain clues about the formation and evolution of the Solar System. NASA's current page says the mission will visit a record-breaking number of asteroids over its long journey.

- Mission overview: [NASA Science — Lucy](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/lucy/)
- Role and program-history source: [UNESCO COMEST directory](https://www.unesco.org/fr/ethics-science-technology/comest?hl=es-PE).


Program classification sources: [NASA — Lucy mission overview](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/lucy/mission-overview/); [NASA Planetary Science Programs](https://science.nasa.gov/planetary-science/programs/); [UNOOSA/APOSCOS conference program (2022)](https://www.unoosa.org/documents/pdf/copuos/stsc/2022/APSCOSymposiumDuringSTSC-ProgramOutline10Feb2022.pdf).
## 4. Venus: international mission and strategy work

A 2022 UNOOSA/APOSCOS conference program identifies Ocampo as Lead Venus Scientist responsible for NASA's collaboration with ESA's Venus Express and JAXA's Venus Climate Orbiter Akatsuki and for Venus Exploration Analysis Group (VEXAG) strategic plans and assessments. NASA en Español's 2022 profile also reports that she was collaborating with ESA on strategic planning for Venus exploration.

This work demonstrates a different type of planetary-science leadership: coordinating scientific priorities and international partnerships for a planet that helps researchers understand atmosphere evolution, runaway greenhouse effects, comparative planetology, and Earth's long-term climate context. The available sources establish the leadership theme but do not provide full project logs or precise dates for each Venus assignment.

Sources: [UNOOSA/APOSCOS program (2022)](https://www.unoosa.org/documents/pdf/copuos/stsc/2022/APSCOSymposiumDuringSTSC-ProgramOutline10Feb2022.pdf); [NASA en Español biography (2022)](https://ciencia.nasa.gov/gente-de-la-nasa/dra-adriana-ocampo-uria-explorando-el-universo-e-inspirando-en-la-tierra/).

## 5. Chicxulub impact structure and Aorounga radar research

Ocampo's research credentials preceded and complemented her program-management roles. Peer-reviewed publications with her name document work on the surface geology of Chicxulub and on the cenote-ring surface expression of the buried crater. Her doctoral thesis also centered on Chicxulub deposits as an analogue for planetary impact ejecta.

In 1996, NASA/JPL reported on a team analyzing Spaceborne Imaging Radar-C/X-SAR observations from Shuttle Endeavour that suggested multiple impact craters around Aorounga in Chad. The report quotes Ocampo and Kevin O. Pope, while explicitly noting that two additional proposed structures had not been scientifically confirmed through fieldwork and that some scientists remained skeptical. That careful wording is part of the scientific record and should be retained when describing the work.

- [NASA/JPL, “Chain of Impact Craters Suspected in Spaceborne Radar Images” (20 March 1996)](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/chain-of-impact-craters-suspected-in-spaceborne-radar-images/)
- [Pope, Ocampo & Duller (1993), “Surficial geology of the Chicxulub impact crater, Yucatan, Mexico”](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00575099)
- [PubMed record: “Surface expression of the Chicxulub crater” (1996)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11539331/)
- [Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam thesis record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/geology-and-emplacement-mechanism-of-chicxulub-crater-deposits-an/)
