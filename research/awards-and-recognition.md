# Awards, Honors, and Recognition

**Research snapshot:** 13 October 2026

The entries below distinguish formal awards from magazine recognition, namesakes, and institutional appointments. Evidence quality varies: some items are documented by NASA, the award organizer, a United Nations program document, or an official university/state listing; some older awards currently rely on reliable secondary biographies and should be confirmed against archival award records if possible.

## Formal awards and named honors

### 1992 — Woman of the Year Award in Science, Comisión Femenil, Los Angeles

A United Nations space-cooperation meeting program and biographical reference entries record this award in 1992. It recognized Ocampo's scientific work at a point when her career was establishing itself in planetary geology.

Sources: [UNOOSA/APOSCOS program PDF (2022)](https://www.unoosa.org/documents/pdf/copuos/stsc/2022/APSCOSymposiumDuringSTSC-ProgramOutline10Feb2022.pdf); [Encyclopedia.com biographical entry](https://www.encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/ocampo-adriana-c-1955-planetary-geologist).

**Evidence status:** documented in institutional meeting material, but an original award announcement was not located in this search.

### 1996 — JPL Advisory Council for Women Award

Encyclopedia.com records this Jet Propulsion Laboratory award and says it related to outreach and community work. NASA's current public biography does not list the award.

Source: [Encyclopedia.com — Adriana C. Ocampo](https://www.encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/ocampo-adriana-c-1955-planetary-geologist).

**Evidence status:** secondary biographical source; needs an archived JPL source for primary-source confirmation.

### 1997 — Science and Technology Award, Chicano Federation

The same biographical reference records this award. A separate original announcement was not located.

Source: [Encyclopedia.com — Adriana C. Ocampo](https://www.encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/ocampo-adriana-c-1955-planetary-geologist).

**Evidence status:** secondary biographical source; retain with transparent attribution.

### 2002 — Included in Discover magazine's “50 Most Important Women in Science”

The 2002 *Discover* feature was a magazine selection of 50 women across science. The UNOOSA/APOSCOS program identifies Ocampo as one of the women included. This should be described as editorial recognition, not a formal prize or an award conferred by a scientific society.

Sources: [Discover, “The 50 Most Important Women in Science” (1 Nov 2002)](https://www.discovermagazine.com/the-50-most-important-women-in-science-5574); [UNOOSA/APOSCOS program PDF](https://www.unoosa.org/documents/pdf/copuos/stsc/2022/APSCOSymposiumDuringSTSC-ProgramOutline10Feb2022.pdf).

### 2016 — National Hispanic Scientist of the Year

The Museum of Science & Industry (MOSI) announced Ocampo as the 2016 National Hispanic Scientist of the Year. The award recognized her planetary-geology work, science leadership, and role in encouraging the next generation of scientists. Tampa Sister Cities also recorded her recognition and a Barranquilla-linked civic honor in October 2016.

Sources:
- [MOSI announcement distributed by PRWeb, 3 Aug 2016](https://www.prweb.com/releases/dr_adriana_ocampo_a_leader_on_nasa_s_juno_mission_named_2016_national_hispanic_scientist_of_the_year/prweb13592650.htm).
- [Tampa Sister Cities, awardee page](https://tampasistercities.org/tscawards/dr-adriana-ocampo/).
- [Tampa Sister Cities report, 22 Oct 2016](https://tampasistercities.org/news/tampa-barranquilla-sister-cities-committee-honors-national-hispanic-scientist-of-the-year-winner-dr-adriana-ocampo/).

### 2022 — Latin America Lifetime Award

NASA en Español's profile published on 29 March 2022 states that Ocampo received the Latin America Lifetime Award, presented by Lifetime to honor women whose work inspires and represents Latin American women globally. The NASA article describes her as the first woman from the space-science field to receive the recognition.

Source: [NASA en Español, “Dra. Adriana Ocampo Uría: explorando el universo e inspirando en la Tierra”](https://ciencia.nasa.gov/gente-de-la-nasa/dra-adriana-ocampo-uria-explorando-el-universo-e-inspirando-en-la-tierra/).

**Evidence status:** reported by NASA; the original Lifetime award page or event page would be useful supplementary evidence.

## Named asteroid: 177120 Ocampo Uría

The minor planet 177120 Ocampo Uría was named in recognition of her service to planetary exploration, including work associated with the New Horizons mission. The UNOOSA/APOSCOS program says the asteroid was discovered by astronomer Marc Buie at Kitt Peak National Observatory in 2003 and subsequently named for Ocampo.

This is a distinctive recognition because the honor is embedded in the astronomical naming record. It is not a conventional trophy or grant, and the dossier lists it separately from formal awards.

Source: [UNOOSA/APOSCOS program PDF (2022)](https://www.unoosa.org/documents/pdf/copuos/stsc/2022/APSCOSymposiumDuringSTSC-ProgramOutline10Feb2022.pdf).

**Recommended follow-up:** obtain and cite the naming citation from the Minor Planet Center's official database or the original minor-planet circular. The current file uses the UNOOSA meeting profile because a direct naming-citation page was not confirmed during this search.

## Other distinctions and institutional service

### Colombian Academy of Exact, Physical and Natural Sciences

UNESCO's current COMEST directory lists her as a member of the Colombian Academy of Exact, Physical and Natural Sciences. This is a professional distinction/affiliation rather than a prize. An academy membership announcement or member profile should be added as primary corroboration.

Source: [UNESCO COMEST membership directory, updated 25 Sep 2026](https://www.unesco.org/fr/ethics-science-technology/comest?hl=es-PE).

### UNESCO COMEST Vice-President, 2026–2027

The directory lists Ocampo as Vice-President of UNESCO's World Commission on the Ethics of Scientific Knowledge and Technology (COMEST) for the 2026–2027 term. This is a current leadership appointment, not an award for lifetime achievement. COMEST addresses ethical questions in science and technology, including ethics of space exploration and utilization.

Sources: [UNESCO COMEST directory](https://www.unesco.org/fr/ethics-science-technology/comest?hl=es-PE); [UNESCO overview of COMEST](https://www.unesco.org/en/ethics-science-technology).

### Courtesy Research Professor at University of Central Florida

UCF lists Ocampo as Courtesy Research Professor in its Department of Physics and Astronomy. This appointment records ongoing academic affiliation and research engagement, not an award.

Source: [UCF Physics — Adriana C Ocampo Uría](https://sciences.ucf.edu/physics/person/adriana-c-ocampo-uria/).

UNESCO's current directory names her as founder and president of the foundation. The Florida Division of Corporations lists the nonprofit entity as active, and names Adriana C. Ocampo in an officer/director role. These sources establish the institutional connection, not yet the full program outcomes.

Sources: [UNESCO COMEST directory](https://www.unesco.org/fr/ethics-science-technology/comest?hl=es-PE); [Florida Division of Corporations — entity record](https://search.sunbiz.org/Inquiry/corporationsearch/SearchResultDetail?aggregateId=fornp-f25000003992-365756b8-59a9-44ed-9e78-57f7ec5f6be5&directionType=PreviousList&inquirytype=EntityName&listNameOrder=STELOGISTICSOLUTIONS+L250000482710&searchNameOrder=STELPOLVOOCAMPOURIAFOUNDATION+F250000039920&searchTerm=STELLINK+FLORIDA%2C+LLC).
