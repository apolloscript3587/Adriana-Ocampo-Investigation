# Methodology, Confidence, and Open Questions

## Scope and research method

This dossier was prepared as a source-led investigation of Dr. Adriana C. Ocampo Uría, born in Barranquilla, Colombia, with emphasis on education, career development, earth-impact research, planetary missions, awards, current affiliations and science outreach. The information snapshot is 13 October 2026.

The research process prioritized:
1. NASA and JPL institutional biographies and mission pages.
2. UNESCO, UN space-cooperation documents and current university faculty records.
3. Peer-reviewed papers and university thesis repositories.
4. Contemporary award-organizer and civic-event announcements.
5. Secondary biographical sources as leads and corroboration where primary documentation was not readily available.

This is a public-interest biographical and scientific research project. It does not seek private information. Only professional facts, published scientific work, institutional roles and public awards are included.

## Attribution framework

A careful account of an individual working on large NASA missions needs several distinct categories:

- **Research contribution:** a publication, field study, data interpretation or named scientific role directly connected to the person.
- **Program leadership:** responsibility for a mission program, including oversight, planning, resource decisions and institutional coordination.
- **Mission outcome:** a result produced by spacecraft, instruments and scientific teams. The outcome should not automatically be attributed personally to the program executive.
- **Public recognition:** an award, editorial selection, naming of a celestial body or institutional appointment.
- **Current role:** an affiliation supported by the most recent institutional source available at the research date.

The dossier intentionally avoids phrasing such as “Ocampo single-handedly discovered” or “Ocampo personally delivered” mission results, unless there is specific evidence for that level of attribution.

## Evidence grades

### Grade A — Strong primary evidence
Examples: NASA biography for personal background and education; a peer-reviewed paper for scientific contributions; UCF and UNESCO institutional listings for current affiliations; NASA mission pages for spacecraft milestone facts; state registry for legal entity status.

### Grade B — Strong institutional biography, but historical
Examples: UNOOSA conference program and NASA en Español profile describing her NASA program responsibilities at the time they were published. These are strong for historical roles but should not be projected forward to 2026.

### Grade C — Reliable secondary or contemporary reporting
Examples: Encyclopedia.com for older award histories; Tampa Sister Cities and professional-science reporting for recognition context where an original institutional record was not identified.

### Grade D — Leads only / not used to support a central claim
Search snippets, derivative biographies, and unreferenced profile sites may suggest a topic to check, but should not be used as the foundation for important claims.

## Handling conflicts and uncertain facts

### Current NASA employment

There is a genuine conflict in page freshness. NASA's profile still lists her as a NASA Headquarters scientist, but its metadata says it was last updated in November 2024. UNESCO's directory updated in September 2026 calls the NASA positions former and lists UCF/COMEST roles; UCF also lists her as Courtesy Research Professor. The dossier follows the latest dated institutional evidence and does not invent an exact NASA exit date.

### Mission-program classification

The UNESCO COMEST page updated in September 2026 provides a compressed French-language description of Ocampo's former Discovery and New Frontiers roles. A parenthetical in that current listing associates Lucy, Juno, and New Horizons near the Discovery Program entry, while the UNOOSA/APOSCOS conference bio from 2022 explicitly places Juno, New Horizons, and OSIRIS-REx under the New Frontiers Lead Program Executive role. NASA's current Lucy mission overview independently identifies Lucy as the 13th Discovery Program mission. The dossier follows those explicit program sources and records that the latest UNESCO wording is ambiguous. It does not infer a complete assignment history from that parenthetical.

### Chicxulub attribution

NASA's profile uses concise language describing research led by Ocampo. The 1993 peer-reviewed article is co-authored by Pope, Ocampo and Duller, and other geophysics and geological studies contributed to the identification and acceptance of Chicxulub. The dossier credits Ocampo as a significant researcher in the collaborative identification and mapping of the cenote ring and buried structure rather than as the sole discoverer.

### Aorounga

The 1996 NASA/JPL report says that radar data suggested additional candidate impact craters, and explicitly records that those structures were not confirmed by fieldwork at the time. Accordingly, the dossier uses the words “suggested,” “candidate,” or “proposed” rather than presenting the entire crater chain as conclusively established.

### Education and date differences

NASA's bio records the B.S. in 1983 and an M.S. in planetary geology but does not show the master's date. The thesis record at Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam lists the PhD thesis with a date of 25 March 2013. Secondary pages vary in the reported dates. The dossier cites the university repository and does not attempt to reconstruct an exact doctoral enrollment timeline.

### Awards

The 2016 National Hispanic Scientist of the Year recognition is supported by an award-organizer announcement. The 2022 Lifetime award is reported by NASA. The 1992, 1996 and 1997 awards are also listed in institutional conference material or reliable secondary references; however, the original award notices for the 1996 and 1997 awards were not located in this pass. They are included with that source limitation made explicit.

## Open questions for future research

4. **Complete publication record:** What papers, reports and conference abstracts are indexed under Adriana C. Ocampo, Adriana Ocampo Uría and name variants? Which are peer reviewed?
5. **Asteroid-naming citation:** What is the official Minor Planet Center citation for asteroid 177120 Ocampo Uría, and what exact contribution does it cite?
6. **Older award documentation:** Can the original JPL award record and Chicano Federation award announcement be located?

## Search and maintenance protocol

For any future update:
- Recheck institutional profile dates, not only page availability.
- Prefer complete publisher or institutional pages over snippets.
- Add the access date for changed pages.
- Record a source for each claim before adding it to the biography.
- When a claim has only secondary support, label it.
- Avoid including personal details irrelevant to professional life.
- Keep English throughout the dossier, while linking to authoritative Spanish- or French-language institutional pages when needed.
- Add changes as dated repository commits and update the README research snapshot.
