# Outreach, Education, and Broader Impact

## Public-facing scientific communication

Ocampo's public profile includes communicating planetary science to audiences beyond professional researchers. NASA's own biography includes her advice to young people and uses her career as an example of persistence in science. NASA en Español's 2022 feature also highlights barriers she faced as a woman and Latina in planetary science, alongside the importance she assigns to passion, planning and perseverance.

Sources:
- [NASA Science biography](https://science.nasa.gov/people/adriana-ocampo/)
- [NASA en Español profile, published 29 March 2022](https://ciencia.nasa.gov/gente-de-la-nasa/dra-adriana-ocampo-uria-explorando-el-universo-e-inspirando-en-la-tierra/)

## Children's science book: El Mundo de Copocuqu

NASA's Spanish-language educational site lists the illustrated educational book *El Mundo de Copocuqu: La Reina Gravedad y el Rey Masa*, written by Adriana C. Ocampo Uria and illustrated by Eliseo Balaguer Calpe. NASA makes the title discoverable through its Space Place educational resources.

The book is an especially clear example of public engagement: it brings gravity and mass to a younger audience using Spanish-language storytelling and illustration. This adds another dimension to the public record beyond mission leadership and technical research.

Sources:
- [NASA PDF: El Mundo de Copocuqu — La Reina Gravedad y el Rey Masa](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/El_Mundo_de_Copocuqu.pdf)
- [NASA Space Place — Copocuqu resource page](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/search/copocuqu/sp/)

## Mentoring and representation in STEM

The 2016 National Hispanic Scientist of the Year program included a public-facing mentoring experience and outreach to middle-school students. The organizer's announcement connects the award to the importance of encouraging students to consider science and technology careers. Tampa Sister Cities also records recognition associated with the Tampa–Barranquilla relationship, reflecting the cross-border public meaning of Ocampo's career.

Sources:
- [MOSI award announcement](https://www.prweb.com/releases/dr_adriana_ocampo_a_leader_on_nasa_s_juno_mission_named_2016_national_hispanic_scientist_of_the_year/prweb13592650.htm)
- [Tampa Sister Cities award page](https://tampasistercities.org/tscawards/dr-adriana-ocampo/)

Ocampo's career can serve as a role model for women and Latino/Hispanic students in STEM, but that general social significance should not be misreported as a measurable causal outcome. Evidence of actual impact would require program participation numbers, surveys, interviews, or studies of the educational initiatives she supported.

## International and institutional work beyond NASA

The UNESCO COMEST directory updated in September 2026 describes Ocampo as a Vice-President of the Commission for the 2026–2027 term and lists her as a Courtesy Research Professor at UCF. COMEST is an international advisory body dealing with ethical questions in science and technology. Its current work program has included ethical considerations in space exploration and utilization.

This gives a broader context for the evolution of Ocampo's public-service role: her career now appears in institutional listings not only as a mission-science career, but also as academic and international science-governance engagement.

Sources:
- [UNESCO COMEST membership directory](https://www.unesco.org/fr/ethics-science-technology/comest?hl=es-PE)
- [UNESCO: COMEST and ethics of space exploration](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/31st-ordinary-session-ibc/joint-session-ibc-and-igbc/13th-extraordinary-session-comest)
